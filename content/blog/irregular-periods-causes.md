Many women experience an irregular period now and then. Occasional changes are usually harmless, but periods that are consistently irregular, very heavy or very painful deserve attention — they are often the body's early signal of a treatable problem.

## What is a "normal" cycle?

- A cycle is counted from the first day of one period to the first day of the next.
- In adults, a normal cycle is usually **21 to 35 days** long.
- Bleeding normally lasts **2 to 7 days**.
- Cycle length can vary by a few days from month to month — that is normal.

In the first two years after periods begin, and in the years before menopause, cycles are often less regular.

## Common causes of irregular periods

- **PCOS** — one of the most common causes; read our [guide to PCOS](/blog/pcos-symptoms-causes-treatment/).
- **Thyroid problems** — both an underactive and an overactive thyroid can change your cycle.
- **Stress** — emotional or physical stress affects the hormones that control ovulation.
- **Weight changes** — significant weight gain or loss, or very intense exercise.
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding** — always rule out pregnancy if a period is missed.
- **Perimenopause** — the transition years before menopause, usually in the 40s.
- **Fibroids, polyps or endometriosis** — can cause heavy or painful bleeding.
- **Certain medicines** and contraceptives.

## See a gynecologist if you have

- Cycles consistently shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days
- No period for 3 months (and a negative pregnancy test)
- Bleeding that soaks a pad every hour, or lasts more than 7 days
- Large clots, or feeling weak and dizzy during periods
- Bleeding between periods or after intercourse
- Severe period pain that stops you from daily activities
- **Any bleeding after menopause** — this always needs to be checked

## What to expect at your visit

Your doctor will ask about your cycle, symptoms and medical history. Keeping a record of your period dates for 2–3 months (on paper or in an app) is very helpful. Tests may include a pregnancy test, blood tests for haemoglobin, thyroid and hormones, and a pelvic ultrasound.

## Supporting healthy cycles

- Regular sleep and meal timings
- Balanced diet with enough iron and protein
- Regular physical activity and yoga
- Stress management through pranayama and meditation
- Maintaining a healthy weight
