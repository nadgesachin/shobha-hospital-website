Nausea, tiredness, mild back pain and swollen feet are common parts of pregnancy. But some symptoms can signal a problem that needs care **the same day**. Knowing these signs — and acting quickly — protects both mother and baby.

> **In an emergency, do not wait for your next appointment.** Call your doctor or go to the nearest hospital straight away. Shobha Hospital emergency: **+91 97142 06386**.

## Go to hospital immediately if you have

- **Vaginal bleeding** — any amount in the second or third trimester, or heavy bleeding at any stage
- **Severe or constant abdominal pain**
- **Fluid leaking or gushing** from the vagina (possible breaking of waters), especially before 37 weeks
- **Baby moving less than usual** in the third trimester, or not moving at all
- **Severe headache** that does not go away, blurred vision, seeing spots or flashing lights
- **Sudden swelling** of the face, hands or around the eyes
- **Fits (convulsions)** or fainting
- **High fever** (100.4°F / 38°C or more), or fever with chills
- **Difficulty breathing, chest pain** or a racing heartbeat
- **Regular painful contractions** before 37 weeks

## Call your doctor the same day if you notice

- Burning or pain while passing urine
- Persistent vomiting — unable to keep food or water down for 24 hours
- Severe itching all over the body, especially palms and soles, in late pregnancy
- Pain, redness or swelling in one leg
- Thoughts of harming yourself, or feeling hopeless most of the day

## Understanding baby's movements

From around 24–28 weeks, you should feel your baby move every day. There is no "normal" number of kicks — what matters is **your baby's usual pattern**. If movements slow down, stop or change, lie on your left side, focus on the movements for an hour, and contact your doctor if you are worried. Do not wait until the next day.

## Why these signs matter

Several serious conditions — such as pre-eclampsia (high blood pressure in pregnancy), placental problems, infection and preterm labour — begin with the symptoms above. Early treatment makes a very big difference to outcomes.

## How regular antenatal care helps

Routine check-ups catch many problems before symptoms appear:

- Blood pressure and urine checks at every visit
- Blood tests for anaemia, sugar and thyroid
- Scans at the right weeks to monitor growth and the placenta

> Trust your instincts. If something feels wrong, it is always better to be checked and reassured than to wait.
