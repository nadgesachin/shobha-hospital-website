Infertility is often assumed to be a "woman's problem", but male factors contribute to many cases where couples struggle to conceive. The good news: sperm are produced continuously, and a new batch takes roughly **three months** to develop — so healthy changes can show results within a few months.

## 1. Smoking and tobacco

Smoking cigarettes or bidis and chewing tobacco or gutkha are linked to lower sperm count, poorer movement and more DNA damage. Quitting is one of the most effective steps a man can take.

## 2. Alcohol

Heavy or regular drinking lowers testosterone and sperm quality. Cutting down — or stopping while trying to conceive — is recommended.

## 3. Weight

Obesity changes hormone levels and is associated with lower sperm counts. A balanced diet and regular exercise to reach a healthy weight helps.

## 4. Heat

The testes work best slightly below body temperature. Avoid:

- Keeping a laptop directly on the lap for long periods
- Frequent hot baths or saunas
- Very tight underwear
- Long hours of sitting, especially in hot conditions (e.g. long-distance driving)

## 5. Diet

A diet rich in vegetables, fruits, whole grains, nuts, seeds and protein provides antioxidants, zinc and folate that support sperm health. Limit processed foods, sugary drinks and fried snacks.

## 6. Stress and sleep

Long-term stress and poor sleep affect hormones. Regular sleep of 7–8 hours, exercise, yoga and pranayama help.

## 7. Occupational exposures

Exposure to pesticides, heavy metals, solvents and industrial chemicals can affect fertility. Use protective equipment at work and mention your occupation to your doctor.

## 8. Medicines and supplements

Anabolic steroids and testosterone supplements taken for bodybuilding can **switch off** sperm production. Always tell your doctor about all medicines and supplements you take.

## When should a man get tested?

Both partners should be evaluated together if a couple has been trying for **12 months** without success (or **6 months** if the woman is over 35) — or earlier if there is a known problem such as:

- Previous surgery or injury to the testes or groin
- Undescended testes in childhood, or mumps after puberty
- Problems with erection or ejaculation
- Low sex drive

The first and simplest test is a **semen analysis**, which checks count, movement and shape. Hormone tests may follow.
