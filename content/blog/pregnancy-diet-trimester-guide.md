Good nutrition is one of the most powerful things you can do for your baby's growth and your own energy and recovery. You do not need a complicated diet — a balanced, home-cooked Indian thali, eaten regularly and in the right amounts, covers most needs.

## Key nutrients in pregnancy

- **Folic acid** — protects against neural tube defects. Ideally start a supplement before conception and continue through the first trimester, as advised by your doctor.
- **Iron** — prevents anaemia, which is very common in Indian women. Found in green leafy vegetables, dals, jaggery, dates, ragi and meat. Take it with vitamin C (amla, lemon, guava) for better absorption; avoid tea or coffee with meals.
- **Calcium** — for baby's bones and your own. Milk, curd, paneer, ragi and sesame (til) are good sources.
- **Protein** — dals, pulses, sprouts, paneer, eggs, fish, chicken and soy.
- **Iodine** — use iodised salt.
- **Omega-3 fats** — walnuts, flaxseed and well-cooked low-mercury fish.

Your doctor will prescribe supplements such as folic acid, iron and calcium based on your blood tests — do not start or stop supplements on your own.

## First trimester (weeks 1–13)

Your calorie needs barely increase yet, but nutrient needs do.

- Eat small, frequent meals to manage nausea — dry toast, khakhra or a few biscuits in the morning can help.
- Stay hydrated with water, coconut water, buttermilk and soups.
- Ginger, lemon and cold foods are often easier to tolerate.
- If you cannot keep food or water down for a whole day, see your doctor.

## Second trimester (weeks 14–27)

Appetite usually returns, and baby grows quickly.

- You need roughly 300–350 extra calories a day — about one extra small meal, not "eating for two".
- Focus on iron and calcium; this is when anaemia often appears.
- Add a fruit and a protein source to every meal.
- Your sugar test (GTT) is usually done around 24–28 weeks — limit sweets and sugary drinks.

## Third trimester (weeks 28–40)

- Eat smaller meals more often — acidity and fullness are common as baby grows.
- Fibre (vegetables, fruits, whole grains) and plenty of water help prevent constipation.
- Avoid lying down immediately after eating.
- Keep up protein and iron in preparation for delivery and breastfeeding.

## Foods and habits to avoid

- **Alcohol and tobacco** — no safe amount in pregnancy.
- **Raw or undercooked** meat, eggs and seafood.
- **Unpasteurised** milk and cheese made from it.
- **Unwashed** fruits and vegetables, and street food of uncertain hygiene.
- **Too much caffeine** — keep it under about 200 mg a day (roughly two small cups of coffee).
- **Herbal remedies and "pregnancy powders"** not prescribed by your doctor.

## A simple day's plan

- **Early morning:** water, a few soaked almonds or dates
- **Breakfast:** poha / upma / vegetable paratha with curd
- **Mid-morning:** a seasonal fruit or a glass of buttermilk
- **Lunch:** roti or rice, dal, sabzi, salad, curd
- **Evening:** roasted chana, sprouts chaat or milk
- **Dinner:** a lighter version of lunch, eaten 2 hours before bed

> Every pregnancy is different. Conditions such as diabetes, thyroid disorders, anaemia or high blood pressure need a personalised diet plan — ask your doctor or our team for one.
