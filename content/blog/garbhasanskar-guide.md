*Garbha* means womb and *sanskar* means positive influence or nurturing. Garbhasanskar is a traditional Indian practice that focuses on the physical, mental and emotional wellbeing of the mother during pregnancy — with the understanding that a calm, healthy and well-nourished mother provides the best environment for her baby.

## What does a Garbhasanskar programme include?

### Nutrition (Garbhini Paricharya)

Ayurveda describes a month-by-month diet and routine for pregnancy. Today these principles are combined with modern nutrition science: fresh, home-cooked, easily digestible meals, adequate protein, iron and calcium, and regular meal timings.

### Antenatal yoga and breathing

Gentle, pregnancy-safe yoga postures and pranayama help with back pain, sleep, breathing, posture and preparation for labour. These should be learnt from a trained instructor and adapted to each trimester.

### Meditation and music

Meditation, chanting and calming music help reduce stress and anxiety. Lower stress supports better sleep, healthier blood pressure and a more positive pregnancy experience.

### Bonding with the baby

From the second trimester onwards, babies can sense sound and movement. Talking, reading and singing to your baby, and involving the father and family, build an early emotional bond.

### Positive thinking and parenting preparation

Reading uplifting books, practising gratitude, and learning about breastfeeding, newborn care and parenting help parents feel confident and prepared.

## What Garbhasanskar is — and what it is not

- It **is** a holistic wellness practice that supports the mother's emotional and physical health.
- It **is not** a substitute for antenatal check-ups, scans, blood tests or medicines prescribed by your doctor.
- It **should not** involve unprescribed herbal medicines or practices that are unsafe in pregnancy.

## When can you start?

Ideally the journey begins even before conception, with preconception counselling, a healthy diet and stress management. Most structured programmes start in the first or second trimester once pregnancy is confirmed and your doctor has cleared you for yoga and other activities.

> Every pregnancy is unique. Check with your obstetrician before starting any new exercise, diet or wellness programme.
