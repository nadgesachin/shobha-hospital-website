Ultrasound scans are safe, painless and one of the most important parts of antenatal care. Each scan is timed to answer specific questions about your baby's development. Here is what to expect at each stage.

## 1. Early pregnancy scan (6–8 weeks)

- Confirms the pregnancy is inside the uterus (and not ectopic)
- Detects the baby's heartbeat
- Checks whether there is one baby or more
- Dates the pregnancy accurately — this date is used for the rest of your pregnancy

## 2. NT scan (11–13 weeks + 6 days)

The nuchal translucency (NT) scan measures a small fluid space at the back of the baby's neck. Combined with a blood test, it estimates the chance of chromosomal conditions such as Down syndrome. It also gives an early look at the baby's structure.

> The NT scan has a narrow time window. If you miss it, it cannot be done later — plan it in advance.

## 3. Anomaly scan / Level-2 scan (18–20 weeks)

This is the most detailed scan of pregnancy. The sonologist checks the baby's brain, face, spine, heart, abdomen, kidneys and limbs, as well as the position of the placenta and the amount of fluid. Most babies are perfectly healthy — but if a concern is found, early knowledge allows the best possible planning.

## 4. Growth scans (around 28, 32 and 36 weeks)

These scans track how well your baby is growing and check:

- Baby's estimated weight and growth curve
- Amniotic fluid levels
- Placenta position and health
- Baby's position (head-down or breech) as delivery approaches

Not every woman needs every growth scan — your doctor will advise based on your pregnancy.

## 5. Doppler studies (when needed)

A Doppler scan measures blood flow through the umbilical cord and the baby's blood vessels. It is used in higher-risk pregnancies — for example with high blood pressure, diabetes, twins, or when the baby seems small — to make sure the baby is getting enough oxygen and nutrition.

## Blood tests that go alongside

- Blood group, Rh factor and complete blood count
- Thyroid profile and blood sugar (HbA1c in early pregnancy)
- Glucose tolerance test (GTT) around 24–28 weeks
- Screening for infections and urine tests

> Is ultrasound safe? Yes — diagnostic ultrasound uses sound waves, not radiation, and is considered safe when performed for medical reasons by trained professionals.
